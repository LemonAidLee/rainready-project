# After the Water: Who Does What When a Township Floods

**Written for:** the team, and as the source for the website's "After the water" page (`#/after`), the Brief
section and the deck slide. **As of:** 21 October 2026. **Builds on:**
[THREE_CHALLENGES_REVIEW.md](THREE_CHALLENGES_REVIEW.md) section 2 (wins over it where they differ) and
[RESPONSIBILITY_RECORD.md](RESPONSIBILITY_RECORD.md).

House rule: **who holds a duty under the published rules, never who is to blame.** Not legal advice.

## 1. In four lines

1. **Nobody pays automatically.** What arrives on its own is relief: a cash aid per household, not compensation.
2. **Your own insurance is the first line**, and only if it covers flood. Most households and cars are not covered.
3. **Relief after the late-2025 floods was RM1,000 per household from the federal government and RM1,000 per family
   from Selangor.** Klang had the most families paid.
4. **Compensation for a loss is decided by a court.** On 29 September 2026 a High Court held public authorities liable
   for a Selangor flood. It is not final.

## 3. The Taman Sri Muda ruling (29 September 2026)

- **Court:** Shah Alam High Court, Judicial Commissioner Dr Shoba Rajah. **Claimants:** 50 Taman Sri Muda residents,
  over the December 2021 flood. **Special damages sought:** RM3,767,148.51, plus general and aggravated damages
  ([Media Selangor](https://mediaselangor.com/en/2026/09/398538)).
- **Held liable:** the federal government (the court found breaches by NADMA and METMalaysia), the Selangor JPS
  director and Shah Alam City Council (MBSA), jointly ([The Star](https://www.thestar.com.my/news/nation/2026/09/30/authorities-held-liable-over-2021-floods);
  [FMT](https://www.freemalaysiatoday.com/category/nation/2026/09/29/taman-sri-muda-residents-win-suit-against-govt-others-over-2021-floods)).
- **Why:** each owed an **operational duty**. JPS to run and maintain the flood assets it controls; **MBSA to repair,
  clear and maintain local drains, although JPS owns the main flood works**; NADMA and MET to warn and respond
  competently.
- **"Act of God" rejected:** it "is inapplicable where reasonable human intervention was available" (FMT). Rain above
  the design standard was not enough on its own (The Star).
- **Not final:** damages not yet assessed (case management on 6 Oct 2026); Selangor said it would take legal advice
  before deciding whether to appeal ([Malay Mail](https://www.malaymail.com/news/malaysia/2026/09/30/selangor-mulls-appeal-after-high-court-finds-authorities-liable-for-2021-taman-sri-muda-floods/237147)).
  **[verify]** appeal and damages: no report found as of 21 Oct 2026.
- **Limits for BBR:** Sri Muda was a river and pump flood, not a township drainage flood. It shows how duties are
  read, not what would happen at BBR. Councils keep a statutory immunity for **approving** building plans (s. 95(2),
  Street, Drainage and Building Act 1974, upheld in Highland Towers, Federal Court 2006,
  [HBA archive](https://hba.org.my/archive/focus/HT/2006/council_not.htm)), so maintenance is the stronger ground.
  **[verify] with a law lecturer before a slide relies on the approval vs maintenance distinction.**

## 5. What a resident can do, in order

1. **Record the damage** before cleaning: dated photos, a list of what was lost, receipts. (RainReady's damage record,
   concept feature 8, is with the resident door work, not built yet.)
2. **Register** with the relief centre or the district office, for the federal and state aid.
3. **Claim** on any insurance that covers flood.
4. **Report** the failed drain or pond to its holder's official channel (iClean Selangor or Sispaa for council
   drains).
5. **Act together** through the residents' association: a collective record carries more weight, as the 2024
   memorandum and the Sri Muda claim both show.

## 7. Slide text: "After the water: who does what" (deck or appendix)

> **No one pays automatically.** Relief first: RM1,000 per household (federal) and RM1,000 per family (Selangor,
> late-2025 floods). Insurance only where a policy covers flood: most homes and cars are not covered. Compensation
> only through a court: on 29 Sep 2026 the High Court held the federal government, JPS Selangor and MBSA liable for
> the 2021 Taman Sri Muda flood, on their duties to maintain drains and to warn (not final).
>
> **At Bukit Raja,** the duty for each drain and pond sits with MBDK, MBSA, JPS or, for the two ponds, is **not
> settled** on public record. RainReady shows who holds each duty, never who is to blame, so residents know where to
> go and each party can show the upkeep it did.
