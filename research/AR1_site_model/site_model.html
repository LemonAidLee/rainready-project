What to model, and how much to trust it

H / M / L = confidence

Build these, in this order

  1. Ground plane 1220 × 2450 m, almost flat, falling gently to the south-south-west M. Do not sculpt hills inside the block.
  2. Two retention ponds — A at 5.6 ha, B at 5.7 ha, both near Jalan Makyong and Persiaran Batu Belah H.
  3. Road network — 487 mapped ways. Jalan Makyong, Jalan Sumazau and Persiaran Batu Belah are the streets the 2024 memorandum named H.
  4. Housing blocks — 415 terraced houses at about 99 m² each, in rows along the Makyong and Sumazau grids H for position, L for height.
  5. Shoplots and commercial — 116 retail units near 198 m², 14 larger commercial boxes, one hospital M.
  6. Drains and ditches — 65 mapped lines. Section and depth are unknown L.
  7. Council line — enters the east edge, runs west then turns north M.
  8. The north-east lake — landscaped, with mown banks, but only a ~60 m western sliver is inside the frame. OpenStreetMap gives it no name and tags it a flood retention pond, so “Tasik Setia Alam” is our label, not a source's H.

Where the ground data is weak

  • Two global DEMs disagree. The 90 m DEM behind the site-selection note put this plain at 4–6 m and the ponds at 4 m. The 30 m SRTM used here reads 8–12 m across the same block, with the ponds at 10–11 m.
  • Only the pattern survives both. Ground falls toward the south-south-west at roughly 1.3 m per km, and the high ground sits outside the frame to the south-east. Model that trend, not the absolute metres M.
  • Raw is noise. Point to point the raw grid swings 1–18 m over flat housing. The Raw surface is shown so it is not hidden, not so it can be modelled L.
  • A third DEM made it worse, not better. Copernicus GLO-30, added 11 Oct 2026 as the GLO-30 surface above, sits on average 3.7 m below the SRTM surface, spread 3.4 m. Three sources, three answers. All of them are surface models, so none of them is ground L.
  • Still to settle: FABDEM was unobtainable on 11 Oct 2026 (server down) and is non-commercial. What would actually settle it is a LiDAR or IFSAR DTM. Until then treat ground level as relative, never absolute.
Not a flood model. Nothing here shows flood depth, and the ground data cannot support one. The model is a stage for the physics track to run scenarios over, and for the deck to show how water crosses a council line.

Checked against imagery, 11 Oct 2026

  • Both ponds are real open water. Pond A has been water since at least 2018. Outlines match within about 10–20 m; pond A's polygon runs slightly inside its western edge H for water, M for the outline. Depth is still unknown and cannot be read from imagery.
  • The 216 m² basin is not a pond. Scrub and bare ground on the motorway verge. Do not model water there M.
  • The council line follows the road reserve on its north–south leg; its east–west leg crosses open grass with nothing visible on it, running parallel to a drain M. Exactly one mapped drain and two roads cross it.
  • The main ditch is engineered — a concrete-lined trapezoidal channel about 8–10 m wide at 3.0976 N, 101.4337 E. The only capacity clue the project has M.
  • Buildings really are missing. Whole estates in the west and north-west of the block carry no footprints: the 633 mapped are an estimated 40–60% of what stands. Model generic housing in the gaps and say so H.
  • Storeys could not be counted — the imagery is near-nadir with no obliques and no published capture time. Heights stay assumed. Street View is the way to settle it.
  • Wetland Townpark: none of the water east of the extent looks like it. They are unlandscaped detention basins and wastewater ponds. Still unlocated.
  • No usable catchment yet. Flow routing over the 30 m DEM was tried and thrown out: the surface model climbs 8.5 m over the motorway embankment where the ditch passes under it, so the modelled water never follows the real channel.
