# Evidence Analysis: Is Mobility/Walkability the Best Problem to Target?

Prepared 10 October 2026 from the 18-source walkability pack plus external searches. Proposal deadline: **23 October 2026, 11:59 p.m.** (13 days).

**Review depth.** Sources 1, 3 (findings sections), 6, 8, 10, 11, 12, 13 and 7 (abstract) were read from the files. Sources 4, 9, 16, 17 and 18 are analyst records only (no primary text). Sources 2, 5, 14 and 15 are catalogue/abstract snapshots; 15 (128 pages) was only skimmed. Confidence ratings below reflect this.

---

## 1. Conclusion

Walkability is a **real but moderately evidenced** problem. The pack proves low walking and car dependence nationally, and local studies repeatedly list the same barriers. It does **not** yet prove a large, repeated, quantified unmet need in any one township. Several README descriptions overstate what the files contain (see section 3).

Mobility remains a defensible choice **only if narrowed** to one township, one user group and one barrier cluster. It is not clearly the best problem on evidence alone. It scores best on the combination of evidence breadth, SDP actionability and prototype feasibility in 13 days. Flood/drainage is the strongest-evidenced alternative on scale; maintenance issue-tracking is the best "after handover" fit. Details in section 6.

## 3. Corrections to the pack's own framing

1. **Source 13 contains no findings.** It is a diploma project and the file has only front matter. Do not cite it as direct user evidence.
2. **Source 10 is n=60** and its top-ranked barrier is a pandemic artefact. The README's "frequently reported barriers" overstates it.
3. **Source 8 supports a ~35% perceived shade shortfall**, roughly the same as for public transport and vegetation. It does not single out walking.
4. **Source 12 cuts both ways.** Pedestrians are 5.9% of road deaths; motorcyclists are 68%. Headlining "285 pedestrian deaths" invites the rebuttal that bikers are the bigger road-safety problem.
5. **Source 6 shows people already walk** (50 of 91 walk >30 min/day). That is local use, not suppressed demand.
6. The README's "**5% walking** vs **84% don't walk or cycle for transport**" must not be combined. Different questions, instruments and samples.
7. **Source 7 is a planned-city, government-quarters sample.** Do not generalise.

## 6. Is mobility the best target? Comparison

Qualitative ratings against the judging criteria. Ratings are my judgement, not measurements.

| Candidate | Evidence of repeated pain | People affected | SDP can act | Differentiation headroom | Prototype in 13 days | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A. Walking route quality (shade, continuity, first/last mile)** | Medium | High | Medium-High (township paths) | Medium (audit/prioritisation tools exist) | High (open data + audit) | Elmina already strong; generic "walkability app" looks repackaged |
| **B. Flood / drain early-warning (RainReady)** | High (national, official) | Medium-High | Low-Medium (drains are council/DID) | Medium | Low-Medium (needs drain/sensor data) | No township data; authority sits elsewhere |
| **C. Township maintenance issue triage** | Medium (strata, source 10 maintenance, briefing example) | High | High | Low-Medium (council complaint apps exist) | High | Evidence is indirect; commodity feel |
| **D. Waste / recycling** | Medium | High | Medium | Low (crowded) | High | Many existing apps |
| **E. Neighbourhood connection** | Low-Medium | High | Medium | Medium | Medium | Hard to measure impact |
| **F. Parking / parcels** | Low | Medium | High | Low | High | Weak evidence found |

**Verdict.** A is the best fit if narrowed; B has the best raw evidence but weakest actionability; C is the strongest "after handover" story but least differentiated. I would not switch away from mobility on the current evidence, but I would not claim it is proven best. The case should be built on a site audit, not on the national statistics.

**Narrowing that fits the evidence:** a decision-support tool for SDP and township management that identifies which 500-1,000 m routes between homes, schools/surau and transit stops fail on shade, continuity, lighting or accessibility, ranks fixes by people served, and tracks before/after. That answers who benefits (residents), who acts (SDP) and what to measure. It also reuses the maintenance angle from C.

## 7. Ranked problem statements

1. **Residents of a township** struggle to walk the last 500 m to a station or bus stop because routes lack shade, continuity and lighting, causing car trips for distances people say they would walk. *Evidence: medium. Needs site audit.*
2. **SDP township managers** struggle to decide which pedestrian routes to fix first because no route-level data exists on shade, obstruction and accessibility, causing scattered spending. *Evidence: low-medium (inferred from absence); validate with SDP/management interviews.*
3. **Residents with mobility limitations** struggle to reach everyday destinations because of missing curb ramps and obstructed paths, causing dependence on cars or other people. *Evidence: low-medium (sources 6, 11).*
4. **Residents walking after dark** struggle to feel safe because of poor lighting and weak security, causing trip avoidance. *Evidence: low-medium; hard to validate quickly.*
5. **Residents in flood-prone areas** learn of drain blockages and flood risk too late, causing property loss. *Evidence: high nationally, low locally.*

---

## 8. Five most important gaps

1. No township-level observation for any SDP township.
2. No data on abandoned or rerouted walking trips by barrier.
3. No separation of infrastructure from distance, time, climate and habit as causes.
4. No resident survey of SDP townships on any problem (including alternatives).
5. SDP's own data (complaints, footfall, lighting/shade inventory, transit links) not yet requested.

## 9. Fast validation plan (13 days)

| Days | Action |
|---|---|
| 1-2 | Choose one township and one 500-1,000 m route (home to transit stop/school/surau). Ask SDP/township management for complaint logs and path/lighting inventory. |
| 2-4 | Run a WABSA-style audit (source 6 method) and photograph shade, obstruction, ramps, lighting. Pull open data (OpenStreetMap, satellite land-surface temperature). |
| 4-6 | At most 5 short intercept interviews: which trips do you avoid walking, and why? |
| 6-9 | Build the prototype on the audit data; define 2-3 metrics (e.g. share of route segments meeting shade/continuity/accessibility thresholds; walkers served per fix). |
| 9-12 | Build deck, 5-minute demo, appendix with the evidence table. |
| 12-13 | Test links signed-out; submit. |

**Decision gate (day 4-5):** if the audit and interviews show no consistent failing route, switch to candidate B or C before building.

## 11. Claims NOT yet safe

- "Most Malaysians would walk if infrastructure improved." No survey supports this; Reddit cannot.
- "Heat is the main barrier." Source 8 shows a ~35% shade shortfall, comparable to other items; source 10 is n=60.
- Any percentage combined across studies.
- Anything from source 13 (no results in file) or source 5 beyond the abstract.
- "Walkability causes inactivity."
- The RM300 billion figure (Reddit thread 18).
- Any claim that SDP townships lack walkable paths; Elmina is publicly described as having extensive tracks.
- Any claim that pedestrian deaths are the largest road-safety problem.
