## Study and findings

The explanatory mixed-method study mailed 2,150 questionnaires to a stratified random sample from Kuala Lumpur enumeration blocks. It analysed 478 returns—169 Malay, 164 Chinese, and 145 Indian respondents—and followed with 30 qualitative interviews. Reported barriers included lack of time, hot or otherwise unsupportive weather, unsafe traffic, crime, stray dogs, negative attitudes toward walking, and health constraints. Only 18.9% of Chinese, 37.9% of Malay, and 49.7% of Indian respondents reported reaching the recommended weekly walking level.

## Why it is in this pack

It is direct evidence from the target city that walking behaviour reflects both the built environment and personal/social constraints.

## Evidence caveat

The response rate was low and the data are older. It is strong diagnostic evidence for recurring barriers, but current local validation is still advisable.
