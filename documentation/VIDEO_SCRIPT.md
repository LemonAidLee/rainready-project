# RainReady video: outline and script (draft, 23 Oct 2026)

**Written for:** the team leader and whoever records the voice-over. **Runtime:** about **4:46** (limit 5:00).
**Sources of the pictures:** the story deck ([../presentation/](../presentation/README.md), paper-collage v3) and the
website's guided demo (https://rainready-wheat.vercel.app/#/demo?clean). How to record and edit:
[VIDEO_GUIDE.md](VIDEO_GUIDE.md). This file is the master script; the deck's voice-over panel (**N**) holds the same
slide lines.

Timings assume a calm narrator at about 145 words a minute (634 words in all), with the pauses marked. Every figure is
from the slides, the demo captions or [../research/TBD_VERIFY_RESOLUTION.md](../research/TBD_VERIFY_RESOLUTION.md); none
is new.

---

## 1. Outline

| Act | Time | What the viewer should leave with | Scenes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1. The problem** | 0:00–1:33 | Floods here are real and recurring; the water crosses a council line; everyone is acting, but separately | Cold open · Title · It keeps happening · Meet Aiman · The crossing · The gap · Hand-off |
| **2. The product** | 1:33–3:12 | One shared picture that screens storage, tests fixes, admits its unknowns, and takes residents' reports | Guided demo, 12 steps |
| **3. The case** | 3:12–4:46 | What is new, who gains, why it can work, what we ask for, how far it goes | What's new · Who it helps · Can it work · Pilot · Value · Close |

**The thread:** one person, **Aiman** (an illustrative drainage engineer at a developer), asks "how do I show everyone
their part?" in act 1; the demo answers it in act 2; "Now Aiman can show everyone their part" closes the loop in act 3.

**Judging criteria covered:** Impact (scenes 1, 3, 4, 9) · Innovation (5, 6, 8) · Feasibility (demo, 10, 11) ·
Strategic fit (3, 9, 12) · Commercial value (11, 12) · Evidence (sources on every slide, 10).

## 3. Read-through script (for the voice recording)

One paragraph per file. Record each twice; keep the better take.

**01.** December 2021. In the Klang Valley, one month's rain fell in a single day: 363 millimetres in 24 hours at
Sentul. … In Bandar Bukit Raja, in Klang, residents say the water reached three metres.

**02.** We are RainReady. Property lines stop at the fence. Water doesn't.

**03.** And in Bandar Bukit Raja, it keeps happening. Residents say Jalan Makyong floods three times a year. JPS logged
ponding there two years running. 583 residents petitioned the Menteri Besar. … And Sime Darby Property's managing
director called it a top priority.

**04.** Meet Aiman, a drainage engineer at a township developer. … "Residents ask me why their street floods. I can
show them our ponds. But the water also comes from upstream, under another council. How do I show everyone their part,
before the next storm?"

**05.** Here is Aiman's problem on one map. Rain on Setia Alam, in Shah Alam, crosses the council line in one drain,
and fills a shared drain and ponds A and B in Klang. One system, four holders.

**06.** And it's not that nobody is acting. Both councils, JPS and Sime Darby Property all have real programmes. But they
run separately, with separate records. … What's missing is a shared picture.

**07.** So we built one. Meet RainReady.

**08 (demo).** This is a simulated township based on Bandar Bukit Raja, with Setia Alam upstream. ‖ Here is the council
line, and the one drain that crosses it. ‖ These are the streets residents named in 2024, and where JPS recorded
ponding. ‖ We run Malaysia's own stormwater manual, MSMA 2, for a 100-year storm. Red means storage falls short, not a
flood depth. ‖ Today, pond A must hold about 151 thousand cubic metres, and the shared drain is already full. ‖ Now the
next parcels upstream are built, without full peak control… and the shared drain carries about ten percent more. That
is a difference between scenarios, not blame. ‖ Planners can test a fix before anyone pays for it. A new pond on vacant
land cuts pond A's need by about seventeen percent. ‖ The drain's real size isn't public, so it's a slider, and the
answer moves with it. That is the first thing a pilot measures. ‖ Everything becomes one page, for the meeting where the
developer, both councils and JPS sit at the same table. ‖ And the resident door: report a blocked drain with a photo.
The residents' association checks it and sends it to whoever looks after that place. ‖ Every report lands as a pin on
the same model, so planners see it too. RainReady never issues its own forecast; it builds on official warnings.

(‖ = a new demo step: leave 1–2 seconds of silence, so the editor can cut there.)

**09.** What's new? Today, each body keeps its own records. RainReady puts both councils, the developer and JPS in one
picture: storage screened live, residents' reports on the same model, who maintains what, and every assumption sourced.
Each part exists somewhere. … The combination is new.

**10.** Now Aiman can show everyone their part: the developer, both councils, JPS, and the residents themselves.

**11.** And it works today, on public data. It agrees with JPS's own flood record and with an independent flood model,
and three more townships were set up from open data. We show risk bands, never depths, and unknowns are sliders. … But
every limit closes with records that already exist: survey terrain, the drainage records, the street-drain layout.
Getting them is the pilot's first three months. Decision support, not prediction.

**12.** We propose a 12-month pilot in Bandar Bukit Raja: real drain and pond data first, then both doors open, then one
Brief at one joint meeting. … Our ask: access to the records, and the pilot.

**13.** And it travels: the same method can screen any of Sime Darby Property's 28 townships.

**14.** Planners see where storage falls short. Residents act in time. And their reports shape the next plan. …
RainReady. Property lines stop at the fence. … Water doesn't.

## 4. Before recording: checks

- **Numbers in the demo.** Steps 6, 8 and 9 say 151 thousand m³, about 10 % and about 17 %. They are read live from the
  model on screen; if a caption shows something else, say what the caption shows.
- **Pronunciation.** Makyong (MAK-yong), Menteri Besar, Setia Alam, MSMA ("M-S-M-A two"), JPS ("J-P-S").
- **Timing.** At 145 words a minute this runs 4:46. If a take runs long, cut in this order: demo step 7 (−5 s), the
  sentence "That is the first thing a pilot measures." (−3 s), demo step 3 (−6 s).
- **House rules.** No predicted depths (the 3 m is the residents' account of 2021, and slide 1 says so); "a difference,
  not blame"; Aiman is illustrative; the resident door builds on official warnings.
