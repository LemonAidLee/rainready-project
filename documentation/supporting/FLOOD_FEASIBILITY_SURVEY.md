# Flood Idea: Data, Tools and Feasibility Survey

**Idea under test:** a shared, up-to-date view of how water moves across council and developer boundaries in a flood-prone township, with four features: (1) a catchment view linking upstream development to downstream flooding, (2) a retention-pond capacity and "what-if" check, (3) a responsibility map of drains, ponds and river reaches, (4) pre-storm alerts for specific streets.
**Reference site:** Bandar Bukit Raja (BBR), Klang, with upstream Setia Alam (Shah Alam). See [TOWNSHIP_GAP_ANALYSIS.md](TOWNSHIP_GAP_ANALYSIS.md).
**Method:** online search only. Items marked *(background)* come from general knowledge, not from this session's searches, and must be verified.

---

## 1. Verdict

| Question                     | Answer                                                                                                                 |
| ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Is public data sufficient?   | **Enough for a screening-level prototype with stated assumptions. Not enough for engineering-grade prediction.** |
| Is it realisable before the deadline? | **Yes, if scoped to one sub-catchment (Setia Alam to BBR Phase 1-2) and built on simplified methods.**           |
| What is missing?             | Drain network, pond geometry, ownership records and calibration data. None were found publicly.                        |
| Biggest risks                | Overclaiming accuracy; alerts that look like official warnings; blaming a specific agency                              |

| Feature                                              | What we can really do                                                                                                                                                                                                  | What we cannot                                                                                                                       | Rating                                                                         |
| ---------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **1. Catchment view (upstream to downstream)** | Delineate the Setia Alam to BBR catchment from the terrain model; compare land cover and building footprints; show the flow path and low-lying zones; overlay the documented flooded streets                           | Predict flood depth or timing; resolve drains smaller than the terrain grid; prove that upstream development*caused* a given flood | **Feasible as screening**                                                |
| **2. Retention-pond "what-if"**                | Estimate required storage for a design storm with simple methods (rational method, volume balance, optionally Modified Puls); compare against assumed pond area and depth; show how much an extra pond changes the gap | Know the real pond dimensions (not public; residents say only that they are "shallow"); give design-grade numbers                    | **Feasible with assumptions, strongest fit for the mechanical engineer** |
| **3. Responsibility map**                      | Build the framework (drain, pond, river reach, authority, contact) and fill in what is documented, such as the two councils and JPS                                                                                    | Verify who owns each asset; a wrong entry could blame the wrong agency                                                               | **Partly feasible; needs SDP or council input**                          |
| **4. Street-level pre-storm alerts**           | Take official MET Malaysia warnings, match them to streets in low-lying zones, and push a message (web, Telegram or app)                                                                                               | Predict flooding at street level; replace JPS or MET warnings                                                                        | **Feasible as an advisory layer, not as a forecast**                     |

**An optional hardware demo for the EE team:** a bench-tested ultrasonic water-level sensor with a dashboard, shown on a model pond. It is a demonstration, not a deployment, since you cannot install anything on site.

## 5. Limitations to state openly

1. **Terrain error is the same size as the problem.** About 1 m of vertical error against floods of decimetres means the model ranks areas by relative risk, not by depth.
2. **No drain network or pond geometry.** Pond results are scenarios, not findings.
3. **No calibration data.** Residents' accounts can only check plausibility.
4. **Cause and blame are contested.** Residents cite upstream development and unimproved rivers; SDP cites inadequate natural drainage. The tool should show flow paths and capacity, not assign fault.
5. **Alerts carry liability.** A message that looks like an official flood warning but is wrong could cause harm. Label it clearly as advisory, link to official warnings, and never claim a forecast.
6. **Data access terms.** The official open-data portal's terms and rate limits were not visible, and no official myPublicInfoBanjir API was found. Do not scrape without checking.
7. **Government activity.** Selangor and Federal Territories are investing in ponds, dredging and forecasting. Judges may ask what you add. A defensible answer is a shared township-scale view across boundaries and a pre-construction check, since the joint plan says nothing on data sharing or early warning.
8. **Team skills.** No one has stated hydrology or GIS-hydrology experience beyond the mechanical engineer's hydraulics. Plan for learning time.
9. **No site validation** is possible.
10. **Sensitivity.** The topic is a live public dispute. Use neutral language.

## 6. What to ask SDP (via the official Telegram channel)

1. Pond locations and approximate dimensions in BBR Phase 1-2.
2. Any drainage layout or masterplan drawings that can be shared.
3. Whether a flood-response or monitoring system already exists.
4. Which agency maintains which drain, pond and river reach.
5. Whether publicly sharing a township-scale flood view is acceptable.

If SDP cannot share data, say so and use assumptions clearly labelled.

## 8. Recommended scope

Build tier A for one sub-catchment, with the two sub-features most likely to impress and survive scrutiny:

1. **The what-if on retention capacity**, because it ties directly to the residents' demand for more and deeper ponds in Phase 1.
2. **The responsibility map**, because it targets the blame-shifting the residents describe, filled only with documented facts.

Keep the catchment view as a supporting visual and the alerts as a simple advisory demo.
