## 1. Competition Goal

The challenge asks university teams to **reimagine the township of tomorrow** by solving a real, specific problem under one of two themes: **People** or **Planet**.

SDP's framing is broader than constructing buildings. A home is only the beginning of a resident's experience; the goal is to keep multiplying the value of a township after residents move in.

The expected solution should be:

- Relevant to a real township problem
- Meaningfully impactful and measurable
- Innovative rather than a repackaged existing product
- Feasible to pilot in a township
- Scalable beyond one location or use case
- Supported by research, user validation and a working prototype or demonstration

### SDP strategic context

SDP describes its purpose as **driving real estate as a value multiplier for people, businesses, economies and the planet**. Its 2025–2030 sustainability strategy centres on fostering resilient communities through:

- Energy and low-carbon development
- Urban biodiversity
- Climate resilience
- Community experience

This context should inform the pitch, but the solution must still address **one well-defined problem** rather than attempting to redesign an entire township.

## 2. Challenge Themes

The team must choose **one focus area**.

### Theme 1 — People

> **How might we enable every resident to live healthier, safer, more connected and more fulfilling lives?**

Relevant focus areas:

- Community wellbeing — residents' physical and mental health
- Safety and security — safer environments for all residents
- Accessibility and inclusion — designing for everyone
- Mobility — getting around the township
- Digital living — connected, technology-enabled lifestyles
- Neighbourhood connection — stronger community bonds
- Healthy lifestyles — active, balanced living

Illustrative problems from the briefing:

- Time and stress involved in finding parking
- Difficulty knowing whom to contact or how to track public-asset issues
- Missed deliveries and inefficient parcel collection

The examples are **illustrative, not prescribed topics**. The team should validate its chosen problem with actual or representative users.

### Theme 2 — Planet

> **How might we create townships that regenerate the environment and make sustainable living effortless?**

Relevant focus areas:

- Waste management
- Circular economy
- Energy efficiency
- Water conservation
- Green transport
- Biodiversity
- Climate resilience

Illustrative problems from the briefing:

- Confusion about recycling rules and collection points
- Selecting the most suitable sites for solar installations
- Comparing environmental, cost and performance claims for building materials
- Demonstrating that waste-derived materials are safe and effective

The solution should go beyond awareness and produce a measurable change in behaviour, systems or environmental outcomes.

## 3. Recommended Problem-Solving Process

The briefing recommends the following sequence:

1. **Observe** — study real township life.
2. **Identify** — pinpoint one specific problem.
3. **Understand** — learn the target users' needs and context.
4. **Create** — design a focused solution.
5. **Build** — prototype or demonstrate it.

For every candidate problem, answer:

- Who experiences it?
- How often does it occur?
- What evidence shows it is important?
- What currently happens instead?
- What would measurably improve if it were solved?
- Why is a township or property developer well placed to act?
- Can a small pilot demonstrate the solution's value?

## 6. Pitch Deck Requirements

The briefing expects the deck to cover eight areas:

### The story

1. **Problem** — the specific township difficulty being solved
2. **Target user** — who experiences it and in what context
3. **Solution** — the focused proposed intervention
4. **Innovation** — what is genuinely different from existing alternatives

### The proof

5. **How it works** — workflow, system or user journey
6. **Impact** — measurable resident, community or environmental outcomes
7. **Feasibility** — why it can realistically be piloted and delivered
8. **Implementation** — pilot scope, stakeholders, resources and scale-up path

### Suggested 10-slide structure

This structure is a working recommendation, not an additional official rule:

1. Title and one-sentence value proposition
2. Validated problem and evidence
3. Target user and current journey
4. Solution overview
5. Prototype/user journey demonstration
6. Differentiation and innovation
7. Expected impact and success metrics
8. Feasibility and pilot plan
9. Value to SDP and scaling model
10. Team, ask and closing vision

Move detailed research, technical architecture, financial assumptions and additional evidence to the appendix.

## 7. Judging Criteria

| Criterion | Weight | Question the judges will ask |
|---|---:|---|
| Impact and sustainability value | **25%** | Can the solution create meaningful and measurable resident, community or environmental outcomes? |
| Innovation and differentiation | **20%** | Is there genuine originality or a differentiated approach rather than a repackaged existing solution? |
| Feasibility and deliverability | **20%** | Can it realistically be piloted and implemented in a township environment? |
| Strategic fit and theme relevance | **15%** | Does it clearly address a People or Planet challenge and an SDP township priority? |
| Commercial and value multiplication | **10%** | Can it create value for SDP, township businesses and/or residents and potentially scale? |
| Team, evidence and pitch quality | **10%** | Has the team researched and validated the problem, developed a credible solution and communicated it clearly? |

### Practical scoring strategy

The top three criteria account for **65%** of the score. Every major product decision should therefore answer:

1. What measurable outcome does this improve?
2. What makes the approach meaningfully different?
3. How can SDP pilot it with realistic resources?

## 14. Definition of a Submission-Ready Project

The proposal is ready only when the team can truthfully say:

- [ ] We solve one clearly defined People or Planet problem
- [ ] We have evidence that the problem occurs in a township context
- [ ] We have spoken to or tested assumptions with representative users/stakeholders
- [ ] Our solution is distinguishable from existing alternatives
- [ ] The prototype proves the central interaction or technical claim
- [ ] We have defined measurable success metrics
- [ ] We can describe a realistic township pilot
- [ ] We can explain value to residents, SDP and/or local businesses
- [ ] We have a credible scale-up path
- [ ] The deck, demo video and prototype links work without special access
- [ ] All sources, datasets and reused code are documented
- [ ] Every mandatory submission item is ready before 11:59 p.m. on 23 October
